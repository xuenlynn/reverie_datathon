# Customer Segmentation Report: Behavioral And Spending Personas

## Business Question

How can customers be segmented using behavioral and spending data so that marketing spend can be prioritized and offers personalized, in order to increase marketing ROI?

## Executive Answer

The saved KMeans model predicts customer segment membership using 10 behavioral and spending features: income, total spend, wine and meat spend mix, web purchase ratio, spend per purchase, age, children, tenure, and recency. On the holdout test set, the model assigned 336 customers into three active predicted clusters:

| Predicted cluster | Customers | Share of test set | Avg income | Avg total spend | Response rate | Primary business read |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 0 | 97 | 28.9% | $77,331 | $1,422 | 28.9% | Premium high-value customers |
| 1 | 113 | 33.6% | $31,344 | $130 | 12.4% | Low-spend, price-sensitive customers |
| 2 | 126 | 37.5% | $52,293 | $480 | 6.3% | Mid-value wine-led households |

Marketing spend should be prioritized toward cluster 0 first because the model predicts this group as the highest-value segment: it has the highest average spend, highest income, highest spend per purchase, and strongest campaign response rate in the test predictions. Cluster 2 should receive lower-cost personalized retention and cross-sell offers because it is the largest predicted group but has a weak response rate. Cluster 1 should receive budget-controlled promotional offers because it has the lowest spend and lower expected ROI.

Source data: `data/processed/cluster_summary.csv` and `data/processed/test_with_clusters.csv`, generated by `models/extract_predictions.py` from `models/final_model.sav`.

## Model Prediction Basis

The final model is `KMeans(n_clusters=4)`. Its prediction output is `predicted_cluster`, a segment label from the nearest learned centroid. It does not output purchase probability or campaign probability. The supporting distance fields, such as `distance_to_assigned_cluster`, show how close a customer is to the assigned persona center. Smaller distance means the customer is more typical of that segment.

The holdout prediction file contains these model outputs:

| Cluster | Test customers predicted | Avg distance to assigned cluster |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 0 | 97 | 2.487 |
| 1 | 113 | 2.365 |
| 2 | 126 | 2.281 |
| 3 | 0 | N/A |

Cluster 3 exists in the trained model, but no test customers were predicted into it. Across all processed splits, only one customer is assigned to cluster 3, so it should be treated as an outlier pattern rather than a campaign persona.

## Persona Profiles

### Cluster 0: Premium High-Value Buyers

Cluster 0 is the strongest ROI target. The model predicted 97 of 336 test customers into this segment. These customers have the highest average income at $77,331 and the highest average total spend at $1,422. Their average spend per purchase is $69.31, far above cluster 1 at $11.98 and cluster 2 at $25.93.

Behaviorally, cluster 0 has low average children in household at 0.22 and meaningful wine and meat spend mix, with wine ratio 44.8% and meat ratio 32.2%. It also has the strongest post-hoc campaign response rate in the predicted test data at 28.9%.

Recommended marketing action:

- Prioritize this group for premium bundles, early access, loyalty rewards, wine and meat offers, and higher-value catalog or curated offers.
- This segment can justify higher marketing spend because the model prediction identifies high spending behavior and the observed response rate is materially higher than other clusters.

### Cluster 1: Low-Spend, Price-Sensitive Customers

Cluster 1 is the lowest-value segment in the model predictions. The model assigned 113 of 336 test customers to this group. Average income is $31,344 and average total spend is only $130, the lowest of all active predicted clusters. Average spend per purchase is $11.98 and average total purchases is 8.67.

This segment is younger on average at 37.85 years old and has about 1.01 children per household. Campaign response rate is 12.4%, which is better than cluster 2 but less than half of cluster 0.

Recommended marketing action:

- Use low-cost channels and discount-led offers.
- Avoid expensive premium campaigns unless customers first show increased engagement.
- Test entry-level bundles, coupons, essentials, and web retargeting.

### Cluster 2: Mid-Value Wine-Led Households

Cluster 2 is the largest active predicted segment, with 126 of 336 test customers. Average income is $52,293 and average total spend is $480. This group is older on average at 49.54 years old and has the highest average children count at 1.33.

The strongest behavioral signal is wine preference: wine ratio is 66.1%, much higher than cluster 0 at 44.8% and cluster 1 at 25.7%. Meat ratio is lower at 16.6%. Despite being the largest segment, its post-hoc campaign response rate is only 6.3%, the weakest among active test clusters.

Recommended marketing action:

- Personalize with wine-focused replenishment, seasonal wine offers, and moderate basket-building bundles.
- Keep spend efficient because response is weak.
- Use retention and frequency campaigns rather than expensive acquisition-style promotions.

### Cluster 3: Outlier Meat-Heavy High-Spend Pattern

Cluster 3 is part of the fitted KMeans model, but the test prediction output has zero customers assigned to it. When all processed splits are scored, only one customer falls into this cluster. The centroid has high total spend around $1,679, very high meat ratio around 95.7%, and near-zero web purchase ratio.

Recommended marketing action:

- Do not allocate campaign budget to cluster 3 as a standalone persona.
- Treat it as an outlier or candidate for manual review.
- If more customers appear in this cluster after scoring new data, reassess it as a possible high-value specialty segment.

## ROI Prioritization

Recommended spend order:

| Priority | Segment | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Cluster 0 | Highest spend, highest income, highest spend per purchase, highest response rate at 28.9%. |
| 2 | Cluster 2 | Largest segment and moderate spend, but weak response rate means campaigns should be targeted and low-cost. |
| 3 | Cluster 1 | Low spend and lower income; use discounts and cost-controlled channels. |
| 4 | Cluster 3 | Not campaign-scale because the model predicted no test customers into this cluster. |

The best ROI strategy is not to market equally to all customers. The model prediction separates customers by observed spending and behavioral patterns. Cluster 0 should receive the largest share of marketing investment because it combines high customer value with high response. Cluster 2 should receive personalized wine-led offers but with controlled budget. Cluster 1 should receive lower-cost promotional messaging. Cluster 3 should be monitored but not prioritized.

## Caveats

The model is unsupervised KMeans, so `predicted_cluster` is a segment assignment, not a causal estimate or probability of purchase. Response rates are descriptive post-hoc measurements from the predicted test clusters and should be validated with A/B testing before changing budget allocation at scale.
